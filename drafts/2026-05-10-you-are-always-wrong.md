1. truth is fleeting
2. to think that you've arrived at the truth is wrong

4. the biggest liar is yourself
5. distinguish between what sounds good and what is closer to the truth
6. disclaimers


Truth is fleeting. If you've ever written a research paper before, you'll have noticed that they never end in 100% certainty. It is
baked into the scientific method to always criticize your own work; find limitations, identify biases, and make suggestions for future
authors who wish to build upon your findings.

This is, by far, the best way to bring yourself closer to the truth. However, you must not ever think you are at the truth itself. 

It is essential to the pursuit of the truth to never assume you've arrived at your destination. It is easy to see why:

1. if you are right, yet you think you aren't, you can't get more wrong. 
2. if you're wrong, yet you think you are, you'll never get more right.

it is therefore obvious that if your ultimate goal is to arrive at the truth, then it is globally optimal to never think you have done so.

these ideas are very closely tied to humility. humility can be defined as not just a manner of behaving around other people, but as a more
general virtue of not treating yourself as a source of truth.

how do you do this? you always think of yourself as the biggest fucking liar

it is kind of amazing what kind of insights you can derive when applying this methodology. if any thought comes to mind, follow up
with "why?". you'll find that a lot of your ideas have zero substance. most, if not all of your preconceptions held up by
shoddy reasoning break down easily.

how do you know if you're getting closer to the truth? it should *hurt*. like, physically hurt. your brain is not optimized for truth-seeking, it is optimized for survival.
what feels good is not what is necessarily true, or not even necessarily close to true. pain lives in the same place growth does, and truth is right next door.
